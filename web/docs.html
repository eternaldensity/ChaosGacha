What is this?

Two free static web apps for BronzDeck's Chaos Gacha concept: a gacha roller with the same presets and odds as the desktop app, and an explorable Chaos Tree built from the same entries. No account, no server — everything runs in your browser and your progress stays in localStorage on your device.

Rolling in 30 seconds

Pick a ticket preset (or set a custom min/avg/max), pick a category, press ROLL. Results can be copied as text, and every roll lands in History, which you can export. The spin wheel is just theatre — odds are identical with it off.

Tree in 60 seconds

Generate a tree (or open an example), then: 1. the Origin root is free and already unlocked; 2. award yourself tickets — each gives 1 core plus points for a shared wallet; 3. unlock glowing frontier nodes (⬡ marks the ones you can afford right now); 4. spend special tickets (skip / jump / hop) to reach distant nodes; 5. unlock tree-meta nodes (Sight, Graft, Chaos Die…) for powers like extended sight and free unlocks.

Back up anything you care about with export (Trees tab / History tab). Browser storage can be wiped by the browser at any time.

Choose Your Own Start

The gacha is a story-writing tool first and a roller second. If you're starting a new adventure and want a template — how your character got the gacha, and what they begin with — pick one option from each list, or just take inspiration. It's all optional.

Method of acquisition

How you stumbled onto the Chaos Gacha and got hold of its power.

  • 1 — Patron From Beyond. Found drifting in the wrong afterlife, or noticed by an outer god who gifted you the gacha for its own amusement before sending you to a world of its liking. Entertain it well enough and you may yet meet your patron face to comprehensible face.
  • 2 — The Entertainer. Chosen as entertainment by beings beyond understanding, handed the gacha to sow chaos and change the world. No deadlines — with the gacha, entertaining results are guaranteed.
  • 3 — Information Storm. Died, slipped off the road to the afterlife, and met a massive chaotic storm in deep space that looked like a game bug. Your existence was rewritten by chaotic information, configuring itself as the gacha.
  • 4 — Pure Chance. You hit a one-in-10200 lottery coincidence and connected to the infinite ever-changing chaos at the centre of everything and nothing.
  • 5 — Native Link. Your universe already had one link into the gacha — a semblance, a devil fruit, a sacred gear — and you got your hands on it. Only one existed in all of reality, so good job.
  • 6 — Mysterious Stranger. Won a small "harmless" bet against a stranger in a formal suit and fedora. God or devil, test or whim — either way, you now hold infinite chaos.

Starting options

How you ended up where you ended up — and what tickets you start holding.

  • 1 — Transmigration. Still you, baseline human, fresh body in a new world with maybe some downloaded local knowledge — clothes on your back and the gacha in your soul. Starts with: 3× Random Gold tickets.
  • 2 — Native. You live here; this is all normal to you. Prior training and an established backstory — but less starter pity. Starts with: 1× Random Gold , or 2× Random Silver , or 3× Random Bronze .
  • 3 — Take Over. You woke in a canon character's body with their memories, training, and powers. No starter pity — go earn your own tickets. Starts with: no tickets.
  • 4 — Changed Fate. You were meant to be the fated protagonist with a cheat — instead the multiverse replaced it with the gacha, and there's no canon to lean on. Starts with: 1× Platinum Random + 2× Gold Random , or 1× Triple-Advantage Platinum Random (roll all three, keep the best) .
If your opening rolls can't support the story, reroll a few times until they can — and feel free to adjust the starts and their tickets. The story matters more than the rules.

Tickets & feats

In stories using the gacha, tickets are earned through feats — anything impressive you accomplish. The more impressive the feat relative to your power, the better the ticket. Killing a city-destroying kaiju guarantees a good ticket; whether it's Platinum or Legendary depends on how outmatched you were.

Feats are judged by the weight of the act, not semantics: one action earns one feat, and repeatable deeds cap out (pickpocketing 10 people may earn bronze, 50 may earn silver, but no amount of pickpocketing regulars earns gold).

In these web apps there is no feat judge — you award yourself tickets directly. Feats matter when you take rolled results into a story.

Feat tiers

  • Trash — barely feats at all, given as an insult or tease: showing up on time, losing gracefully.
  • Bronze — notable but unpowered deeds: a fair fight won, wolves hunted, a job landed.
  • Silver — peak-human or low supernatural: impressing crowds, taking a gang hideout, killing a bear bare-handed.
  • Gold — firmly impressive: guarded-bank heists, escaping death sentences, beating city-wrecking foes.
  • Platinum — true accomplishments: unbreakable records escaped, foes that level city blocks beaten.
  • Aluminium — joke-tier quest rewards between gold and platinum: twenty rat pelts, an errand for Pepsi Man.
  • Diamond — extraordinary and rare: saving cities, slaying Nomu-class threats.
  • Legendary — legend-making: stealing Thanos' gauntlet, winning Holy Grail Wars, saving countries.
  • Mythical — shoulder-to-shoulder with gods: besting Sword Saints, pacifying Scion, shaking universes.
  • Divine — supremacy proofs: defeating Scion or Goetia alone, conquering worlds.
  • Wild — off the scale entirely: doing something incomprehensibly random in an extreme situation and coming out on top against all odds anyway.

Ticket anatomy

A ticket has a rarity (which pool it pulls from) and a category (ability, item, skill, trait, familiar — or random, which picks one). Some tickets carry a prefix such as Advantage: roll twice, keep the better. The tree app adds traversal tickets — skip, jump, choice-jump, hop — covered under the Chaos Tree tab.

Which feats earn which categories

  • Ability — displays of power, defeating enemies.
  • Trait — resilience, survival, changing the status quo.
  • Skill — expertise and finesse.
  • Item — acquiring things, heists, treasures.
  • Familiar — companionship, taming, diplomacy.

Overlaps are settled by author discretion — the story matters more than the rules.

Categories

Story rules for what each category means. The apps hand you the entries; they don't enforce slots, energy, or cooldowns.

Abilities

Supernatural powers held in ability slots (one by default, +1 per five abilities) and fired with energy. Power scales with your stats and with rarity — don't expect a rare dispel to beat a legendary fireball. Foreign abilities accept your own energy instead of their native source.

Abilities classify roughly like this: Enhancer, Emitter, Thinker, Tinker (interface/build tech; blackboxed by default), Alteration, Manipulation/Kinesis, Recovery, Mover, Changer, Stranger, Master, Passive.

Traits

Passive or reactive modifiers that cost no energy, take no slots, and can be toggled — luck, resistances, allure, world-tweaks. Race traits are permanent once toggled on (hybridizing you), but can be rerolled beforehand.

Items

Anything that isn't an ability, trait, familiar, or skill: weapons, armour, consumables, potions — even placed structures. Actives may carry a cooldown; consumables may carry a restock timer that starts when you take the item out and ends when you may take another.

Familiars

Independent, distinctly inhuman beings — absolutely loyal and obedient even when grumpy, summonable to and from familiar space where they rest and heal, able to draw on your energy with consent. Standard gear included. Tame a non-human creature and it joins the list with the same benefits. (Digimon here are living creatures that don't evolve; Pokémon can't evolve and use level-up/egg moves unless stated.)

Skills

Pure downloaded knowledge and experience — comfortable after a small headache. Roughly: Basic ≈ 1 year of training, Intermediate ≈ 5, Adept ≈ 10, Expert ≈ 25, Master ≈ 100, Grandmaster ≈ one-in-a-billion talent plus work, Divine ≈ surpass-the-gods.

Rolling

Set a minimum, average, and maximum (or pick a preset ticket), choose a category, and roll. Average is the distribution's centre of gravity: at average 2.0 over many rolls, results cluster in 1.9–2.1.

Ticket presets

Bronze ⌀1.3 · Silver ⌀2.3 · Gold ⌀3.3 · Platinum ⌀4.3 · Diamond ⌀5.3 · Legendary ⌀6.3 · Mythical ⌀7.3 · Divine ⌀8.3 · Transcendent ⌀9.3 (each with a matching min/max window).

The draw first pulls a target rarity, then picks among entries within ±0.2 of it — so a result can rarely land a hair outside your min/max. That's the anti-crash margin working as designed, not a bug.

What you get

Vehicles, locations, and other big entries arrive self-contained and at your disposal: you get the thing itself, functional, without its home setting, apocalypse, or cast unless its description explicitly includes them.

Filters & tickets (this version)

Narrow the pool by text, source checkboxes, rarity band, NSFW/Tech toggles (NSFW hides by default), or skip already-rolled results. The ticket wallet holds tickets you spend one at a time; ×10 does ten quick rolls at once. History, stats, and exports live under their tabs.

Chaos Tree

A generated web of every entry, arranged in a sphere by rarity. The free Origin root starts unlocked; every other node costs 1 core + 10rarity points. Tickets grant 1 core each and pool their points into a shared wallet.

Seeing & unlocking

By default you see and can unlock only nodes adjacent to yours (⬡ marks the ones you can afford right now). Skip N reaches up to N extra hops out; [Type] Jump takes the closest node of a category not touching a chosen node; Choice N Jump lets you pick among the N closest; Hop unlocks a distant node plus one free node on the way.

Tree-meta nodes

Special ability/item/trait entries that grant powers while unlocked: extended sight, name/description/source tracing, full-tree reveals, +% ticket points, Echo double payouts, lock-and-refund, lifelines, recalls, added links, topology shuffles, random unlocks, and core-free (10x points) unlocks within two connections of the root. Full list with rarities is in the trees README.

Saves & versions

Each named tree keeps its own progress automatically. Export a tree + progress as one file; imports accept those or Python-generator tree JSONs. Copy duplicates progress, Fresh restarts the same parameters. Every tree records the data version it was generated with — a mismatch warning means regenerating now could reshape it, so back up first.
